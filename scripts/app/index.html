VOW · Verifiable Operator Warrants

Let an agent approve a purchase. Never let it choose where the money goes.

An agent that can pay needs a spending key, and a spending key can pay anyone. VOW gives the agent permission to release money, and gives it no way to change the destination.

The problem in three lines

  1. Hand an agent a spending key and a modified client can send the budget anywhere.
  2. Put the approved supplier list on chain instead and you publish your whole procurement plan.
  3. VOW publishes a commitment to the permission set, not its contents. The contract holds the money and releases it only to a supplier the owner already approved, under a cap the owner already set, once.

The contract owns the escrow. The agent owns neither the funds nor their spending key.

What is proven today

No VowVault has been declared, deployed or funded on any network. Without a deployment manifest both screens below load in a read-only state and every write control stays disabled. Nothing here claims a mainnet payment.

The vault contract and the client encoding are covered by local contract and TypeScript tests. Each public claim carries its own evidence tier — proposed, local-model, local-contract-test, deployed, mainnet-executed, independently-reproduced — in the claim ledger.
